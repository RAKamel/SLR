This review establishes a task-centred evidence record for LLM-based cybersecurity agents across detection, investigation, response, reporting, and penetration testing. The recorded search and selection process includes 61 studies, while distinguishing operational tasks, agent architecture, adaptation, autonomy, and human oversight.

The verified examples show why implementation scope and evaluation units must accompany performance claims. Recommendations, corrected artifacts, simulated outcomes, and executed actions provide different evidence. Code availability, access to input datasets, and release of the complete experimental record also require separate assessment. These distinctions help researchers design comparable evaluations and help practitioners identify the controls and assumptions behind an autonomy claim.

The review remains constrained by substantial full-text non-retrieval and incomplete verification of AI-prefilled extraction and coding. Category frequencies and corpus-wide interpretations are therefore provisional. Its conclusions should be read with those limitations and the study-level evidence qualifications.
